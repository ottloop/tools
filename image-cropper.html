Introduction to image cropping

Not every photo arrives in the shape you actually need. The OttLoop Image Cropper lets you cut JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, AVIF and SVG images down to the exact frame you want — freeform, locked to a fixed ratio, forced into a perfect square, or clipped into a circle — directly inside your browser, with no software to install and no account to create.

Drag the crop box into position, resize it from any corner, or snap it instantly to a ratio like 1:1, 16:9 or a social media preset. Rotate or flip the image first if it came in sideways, then crop, convert the format, and download. Everything updates live so you always see exactly what you're about to keep.

Every image is processed locally using your browser's own rendering engine. Files are never sent to a remote server, which means your photos stay private and the tool works even on a slow connection — only the page itself needs to load once. Once you're happy with the result, download a single cropped image or grab the entire batch as a ZIP file.

What is an image cropper?

An image cropper is a tool that removes everything outside a chosen selection area, keeping only the pixels inside the box you define. Unlike resizing, which scales the whole image, cropping trims it — useful whenever a photo needs to be reframed, straightened into a specific shape, or have distracting edges removed.

How image cropping works

The tool draws your uploaded image onto an interactive stage and overlays a movable, resizable crop box on top of it. As you drag the box or its corner handles, the tool tracks the selection's position and size relative to the image's real pixel dimensions. When you crop, only the pixel data inside that selection is drawn onto a fresh canvas at full resolution and exported as your new file — nothing outside the box survives.

If you rotate or flip the image first, those transforms are baked in before the crop is applied, so what you see in the preview is exactly what you get in the download. Circle crop works the same way, with an additional round mask applied so the corners outside the circle become transparent in PNG or WEBP output.

Smart Crop explained

Smart Crop automatically centers the crop box on the image at a generous size for whichever aspect ratio is active, giving you a sensible starting selection you can fine-tune or use as-is.

Benefits of cropping images

  • Better composition — remove distracting edges and draw attention to the subject that matters.
  • Exact platform fit — hit the precise aspect ratio a profile picture, banner or thumbnail requires.
  • Smaller files — cropping away unused area naturally shrinks the file alongside any resolution loss.
  • Consistent galleries — batch cropping keeps a whole set of product or portfolio photos framed the same way.
  • Fix framing mistakes — straighten and reframe a shot that was taken slightly off.
  • Custom shapes — circle crop is ideal for avatars, badges and profile icons.

Best aspect ratios explained

SQUARE

1:1

The standard for profile pictures, app icons and Instagram feed posts — equal width and height with no dead space.

WIDESCREEN

16:9

The default shape for video thumbnails, presentation slides and most modern screens and monitors.

PORTRAIT

9:16

Vertical, full-screen mobile format used by Stories, Reels, TikTok and other short-form video platforms.

Crop without losing quality

Cropping alone never degrades image quality, since the pixels that remain inside the selection are copied exactly as they were — nothing is scaled, blurred or recompressed by the crop itself. The only quality factor to watch is the output format and quality slider used when saving, and how small a region you crop from a low-resolution source, since a tiny crop from a small image will naturally have fewer pixels to work with.

Best crop sizes for websites

Hero banners typically read best around a wide 1.91:1 to 2.63:1 ratio, while product and gallery thumbnails almost always look cleanest at a consistent 1:1 or 4:3 across an entire catalog. Cropping every image in a set to the same ratio before upload is one of the simplest ways to make a gallery feel intentional rather than mismatched.

Image cropping tips

  • Enable grid lines mentally using the rule of thirds — position key subjects near where the crop box's guide lines intersect.
  • Lock a fixed ratio before dragging when you know the exact platform requirement, so you never have to nudge it after the fact.
  • Rotate first, then crop — cropping before straightening usually means redoing the selection.
  • Use circle crop with a PNG or WEBP output so the corners stay transparent instead of turning white or black.
  • For batch jobs, enable "Same crop for whole batch" once you're happy with one image's framing.

Common cropping mistakes

  • Cropping too tight — leaving no breathing room around a subject can feel cramped once placed in a layout.
  • Ignoring the platform's real ratio — a freeform crop that "looks right" can still get re-cropped awkwardly by the platform later.
  • Ignoring subject placement — centering every crop regardless of where the subject actually is can waste space.
  • Forgetting rotation — cropping a sideways photo before straightening it forces a redo.
  • Circle crop on JPG — JPG has no transparency, so the corners end up filled with a solid color instead of clear.

Use cases

  • Profile pictures & avatars — square or circle crops ready for any platform.
  • Social media posts — hit the exact recommended ratio for every major platform.
  • E-commerce product photos — consistent, tightly framed images across a catalog.
  • Passport & ID photos — precise ratio cropping before printing or uploading.
  • Website banners & thumbnails — reframe wide photos to fit a hero or card layout.
  • Print projects — crop to standard print ratios like A4 before sending to a printer.

faq

Frequently asked questions.

Everything people ask us before cropping their first image.

Is OttLoop Image Cropper really free?

Yes. OttLoop Image Cropper is 100% free with no hidden limits, no watermark, and no sign-up required.

What image formats are supported?

You can upload and crop JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, AVIF and SVG images, and convert between JPG, PNG, WEBP and AVIF on output.

Will cropping reduce my image quality?

Cropping itself does not reduce quality since no pixels inside the selection are altered, though the output quality slider can still affect the final compressed file.

Can I crop to a fixed aspect ratio?

Yes. Choose a fixed ratio such as 1:1, 4:3, 16:9 or a social media preset, and the crop box will snap to that shape as you resize it.

Can I crop a perfect circle?

Yes. Select Circle as the crop shape and the corners outside the circle are made transparent in PNG or WEBP output.

Do you store my uploaded images?

No. Cropping happens locally in your browser, and any temporary data is cleared automatically once you close or refresh the tab.

Can I crop multiple images at once?

Yes, batch cropping is supported. The same crop shape and position is applied across every uploaded image, and you can download them individually or as a single ZIP file.

Can I rotate or flip an image before cropping?

Yes. Use the rotate left, rotate right, flip horizontal and flip vertical controls before positioning your crop box.

Does cropping remove EXIF or metadata?

By default, EXIF data such as camera details and location is stripped automatically during cropping for smaller files and better privacy.

What is Smart Crop?

Smart Crop automatically centers the crop box on the image at a generous size for the selected aspect ratio, so you get a usable result without manual positioning.

Can I undo a crop adjustment?

Yes. Undo and redo buttons step back and forward through your recent crop box, rotation and flip changes.

Do I need to sign up or install anything?

No account, download or browser extension is required. Everything runs directly on this page.

Can I use this tool on mobile?

Yes, the cropper is fully responsive and works on phones and tablets, including touch dragging of the crop box and pasting images from your clipboard.

Will the cropped image have a watermark?

Never. OttLoop does not add watermarks or branding to any file you crop.

Which format should I choose — JPG, PNG or WEBP?

Use JPG for photographs, PNG for graphics or circle crops that need transparency, and WEBP when you want the smallest file size with modern browser support.

Can I convert the image format while cropping?

Yes. Choose an output format such as JPG, PNG, WEBP or AVIF and OttLoop will convert and crop the image in a single step.
