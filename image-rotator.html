Introduction to image rotation

A photo taken sideways, a scanned document that landed upside down, or a screenshot that needs a quarter turn — these are everyday problems, and the OttLoop Image Rotator solves them directly inside your browser. Rotate JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF and AVIF images by 90°, 180°, 270°, or any custom angle in between, with no software to install and no account to create.

Whether you're straightening a phone photo that ignored orientation metadata, preparing a product image for a listing, fixing a scanned PDF page, or building a design mockup that needs a precisely tilted element, this tool gets you there in a couple of clicks. Use the quick 90°/180° buttons for exact turns, or drag the custom angle slider for fine control down to the degree.

Every image is processed locally using your browser's own rendering engine. Files are never sent to a remote server, which means your photos stay private and the tool works even on a slow connection — only the page itself needs to load once. Once you're happy with the result, download a single rotated image or grab the entire batch as a ZIP file.

What is an image rotator?

An image rotator is a tool that repositions the pixels of an image around a center point, turning the whole picture by a set number of degrees. Standard rotations of 90°, 180° and 270° swap or preserve the image's dimensions cleanly, while a custom angle rotation tilts the image freely and typically adds background space around the newly exposed corners. OttLoop's rotator supports both, alongside horizontal and vertical flipping for a full range of orientation fixes.

How image rotation works

Behind the scenes, OttLoop draws your image onto an HTML canvas, applies a rotation transform around its center, and re-exports the result as a new file in your chosen format. For 90°-step rotations, the canvas dimensions swap to fit the turned image exactly, so no pixels are lost or padded. For custom angles, the canvas is sized to fit the rotated bounding box, and any newly visible corners are filled with either transparency or the background color you choose.

Auto-straighten explained

Many phone cameras save photos in landscape orientation internally and instead store a small EXIF tag telling viewers to display it rotated. Not every app respects that tag. Auto-straighten reads the embedded orientation value and applies the correct rotation permanently, so the image displays upright everywhere, including apps that ignore EXIF data.

Why you should rotate images

Incorrectly oriented images are one of the most common formatting issues in everyday image use — sideways vacation photos, upside-down scanned receipts, or screenshots taken mid-rotation on a tablet. Fixing orientation before you upload, print, or share an image avoids awkward previews, broken layouts in documents, and content that looks unprofessional in a portfolio or on a product page.

EXIF auto-rotation explained

READS METADATA

Orientation tag

Cameras and phones store an orientation value (1–8) in EXIF metadata describing how the sensor was held when the shot was taken.

BAKES IT IN

Permanent fix

Auto-straighten physically rotates the pixel data to match that tag, then removes the now-redundant orientation flag.

UNIVERSAL RESULT

Displays everywhere

Once baked in, the image looks correct in every app, browser and device — even ones that don't read EXIF orientation at all.

Image orientation guide

As a simple rule: use 90° left or right when a photo or scan is sideways, use 180° when an image is completely upside down, and reach for a custom angle when you're straightening a slightly tilted horizon line or aligning a design element to an exact degree. Combine rotation with a horizontal or vertical flip when you need a true mirror image rather than a turn.

Image rotation tips

  • Use the 90°/180° quick buttons whenever possible — they're lossless and don't add any background padding.
  • Reach for a custom angle only when straightening a tilted horizon or aligning to a specific degree; small angles add the least extra canvas space.
  • Choose a background fill color that matches your intended use before rotating by a custom angle onto a non-transparent format like JPG.
  • Run auto-straighten first on phone photos before applying any manual rotation, so you're not compounding two rotations.
  • Batch-rotate images with the same required turn in one pass instead of rotating them one at a time.

Common rotation mistakes

  • Rotating by a custom angle when 90° would do — this unnecessarily adds background padding and canvas size.
  • Ignoring EXIF orientation — an image can look correct in one app and sideways in another until the rotation is baked in.
  • Forgetting the background fill — rotating a JPG by a custom angle without setting a fill color can leave unexpected black corners.
  • Rotating repeatedly in small steps — each additional re-encode on a lossy format can soften detail; rotate once to the final angle instead.
  • Confusing flip with rotate — a flip mirrors the image, while a rotation turns it; use the right control for the result you want.

Use cases

  • Phone photo fixes — straighten sideways or upside-down shots before sharing or printing.
  • Document scanning — correct pages that were fed into a scanner at the wrong orientation.
  • E-commerce photography — align product shots consistently across a catalog.
  • Design & mockups — tilt design elements to a precise custom angle.
  • Social media — fix orientation before posting to avoid awkward auto-cropping.
  • Print preparation — ensure pages and photos are correctly oriented before sending to print.

faq

Frequently asked questions.

Everything people ask us before rotating their first image.

Is OttLoop Image Rotator really free?

Yes. OttLoop Image Rotator is 100% free with no hidden limits, no watermark, and no sign-up required.

What image formats are supported?

You can upload and rotate JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF and AVIF images, and convert between JPG, PNG and WEBP on output.

Will rotating reduce my image quality?

Rotating by 90°, 180° or 270° is lossless since pixels are only repositioned. Custom angle rotation may introduce minor edge smoothing, which the quality slider controls.

Can I rotate by a custom angle instead of 90°?

Yes. Use the angle slider or type any value between 0° and 359° for fine, precise rotation.

Do you store my uploaded images?

No. Rotation happens locally in your browser, and any temporary data is cleared automatically once you close or refresh the tab.

Can I rotate multiple images at once?

Yes, batch rotation is supported. Upload several images together and download them individually or as a single ZIP file.

What's the difference between 90°, 180° and a custom angle?

90° and 270° swap width and height, 180° flips the image upside down without changing dimensions, and a custom angle rotates freely but adds background space around the tilted edges.

Can OttLoop auto-fix orientation from my phone camera?

Yes. Auto-straighten reads the image's embedded EXIF orientation tag and rotates it upright automatically.

Does rotating remove EXIF or metadata?

By default, EXIF data such as camera details and location is stripped automatically after rotation for smaller files and better privacy. You can choose to keep it instead.

Can I crop or resize after rotating?

Yes. Enter a target width or height and OttLoop applies the resize immediately after rotation in the same step.

What happens to transparent backgrounds when I rotate?

PNG and WEBP transparency is preserved by default. For formats without alpha support, like JPG, you can choose a background fill color for the newly exposed corners.

Do I need to sign up or install anything?

No account, download or browser extension is required. Everything runs directly on this page.

Can I use this tool on mobile?

Yes, the rotator is fully responsive and works on phones and tablets, including pasting images from your clipboard.

Will the rotated image have a watermark?

Never. OttLoop does not add watermarks or branding to any file you rotate.

Can I undo a rotation?

Yes. Undo and redo controls step back and forward through your rotation history, and Reset returns the image to its original orientation.

Can I convert the image format while rotating?

Yes. Choose an output format such as JPG, PNG or WEBP and OttLoop will convert and rotate the image in a single step.
