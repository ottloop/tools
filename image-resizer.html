Introduction to image resizing

Uploading a photo straight from a modern camera or phone into a form, website or social post almost always means fighting against the wrong dimensions. The OttLoop Image Resizer fixes this by scaling JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, AVIF and SVG files to any width, height, percentage or ready-made preset — directly inside your browser, with no software to install and no account to create.

Whether you're prepping a thumbnail for YouTube, a cover photo for Facebook, a product shot for an online store, or a passport photo that needs an exact pixel size, this tool gets you there in a few clicks. Type an exact width and height, drag a percentage slider, or tap one of twenty built-in social and print presets, then choose how the image should fit — stretched, fit inside, or cropped to fill the frame.

Every image is processed locally using your browser's own rendering engine. Files are never sent to a remote server, which means your photos stay private and the tool works even on a slow connection — only the page itself needs to load once. Once you're happy with the result, download a single resized image or grab the entire batch as a ZIP file.

What is an image resizer?

An image resizer is a tool that changes the pixel width and height of an image while redrawing its content to match the new dimensions. Unlike simply stretching an image with CSS or HTML display properties, a true resizer re-samples the actual pixel data, so the file you download really is the new size — smaller to upload, faster to load, and correctly dimensioned for wherever it's headed next.

How image resizing works

Resizing works by reading your image onto a canvas at its new target dimensions and letting the browser's resampling algorithm recalculate every pixel in between. When you shrink an image, nearby pixels are blended together; when you enlarge one, new pixels are interpolated from their neighbors. High quality resampling smooths this process so edges and gradients stay clean rather than blocky.

OttLoop also supports three fit modes for when your target dimensions don't match the image's original aspect ratio. Stretch forces the image into the exact box you specify, which can distort it. Fit Inside scales the image down until it fits entirely within the box, leaving it undistorted. Fill Frame scales the image up until it fully covers the box, then crops the overflow — the standard behavior for cover photos and thumbnails.

Smart Resize explained

Smart Resize applies high quality resampling and sensible defaults automatically, so you get a clean, correctly proportioned result without adjusting every setting by hand.

Benefits of resizing images

  • Faster page load times — an image sized for its actual display area loads far quicker than one relying on browser scaling.
  • Correct fit every time — presets for Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn and more guarantee your upload matches the platform's exact requirements.
  • Smaller file sizes — fewer pixels generally means a smaller file, even before any additional compression.
  • Consistent galleries — batch resizing keeps every image in a set at matching dimensions.
  • Print-ready output — DPI and resolution controls prepare images for physical printing, not just screens.
  • Better SEO — correctly sized images are one of the simplest ways to improve Core Web Vitals scores.

Resize without losing quality

Shrinking an image rarely causes a visible quality loss when high quality resampling is enabled, since there's more source detail than the smaller canvas needs. Enlarging is the trickier direction: pushing an image well beyond its native resolution invents pixels that were never captured, which can soften fine detail. As a rule, try to resize down to your target size rather than up, and avoid enlarging small images by more than 150-200% if sharpness matters.

Best image sizes for websites

For a typical blog or marketing site, a full-width hero image rarely needs to exceed 1920px wide, and a blog featured image works well around 1200 × 628. Thumbnails and card images usually look sharp anywhere from 400-800px wide. Resizing down to these ranges before upload — rather than letting a 4000px camera photo be scaled down by the browser on every visit — is one of the fastest ways to shave load time off a page.

Image size vs resolution

Size refers to an image's pixel dimensions — its width and height. Resolution, usually expressed as DPI (dots per inch), matters for print rather than screens, since it determines how tightly those pixels are packed onto physical paper. A 3000×2000px image displayed at 72 DPI looks identical on screen to the same image at 300 DPI; the DPI value only changes how large it prints. OttLoop lets you set both independently — pixel dimensions for how the image displays, and DPI for how it prints.

Image resizing tips

  • Resize to the exact dimensions your platform or layout needs, rather than uploading full resolution and letting it scale automatically.
  • Use Fill Frame for cover photos and thumbnails where cropping to a fixed aspect ratio is expected.
  • Use Fit Inside when the whole subject must stay visible, such as product photography or diagrams.
  • Keep high quality resampling on whenever you're shrinking an image — it's the single biggest factor in a sharp result.
  • Lock the aspect ratio unless you specifically need a stretched result, to avoid accidental distortion.

Common resizing mistakes

  • Enlarging small images too far — pushing a small photo past 200% of its original size often looks soft or blurry.
  • Ignoring aspect ratio — stretching an image to a mismatched box distorts faces, logos and straight lines.
  • Resizing after compressing — always resize first, then compress, to get the smallest file for your target dimensions.
  • Using the wrong fit mode — Stretch on a cover photo or Fill Frame on a diagram can ruin the result; match the mode to the content.
  • Forgetting DPI for print — a screen-ready image at 72 DPI can look pixelated once printed; set 300 DPI for print jobs.

Frequently asked questions.

Everything people ask us before resizing their first image.

Is OttLoop Image Resizer really free?

Yes. OttLoop Image Resizer is 100% free with no hidden limits, no watermark, and no sign-up required.

What image formats are supported?

You can upload and resize JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF, AVIF and SVG images, and convert between JPG, PNG, WEBP and AVIF on output.

Will resizing reduce my image quality?

Enlarging an image beyond its original resolution can soften detail, but shrinking an image with high quality resampling keeps it looking sharp and visually close to the original.

Can I resize by percentage instead of exact pixels?

Yes. Switch to percentage mode and choose any value from 1% to 500% to scale the image up or down proportionally.

Do you store my uploaded images?

No. Resizing happens locally in your browser, and any temporary data is cleared automatically once you close or refresh the tab.

Can I resize multiple images at once?

Yes, batch resizing is supported. Upload several images together and download them individually or as a single ZIP file.

What is the difference between Fit Inside, Fill Frame and Stretch?

Fit Inside scales the image to fit within your dimensions without cropping, Fill Frame scales and crops to exactly fill the frame, and Stretch forces the image into the exact size even if the aspect ratio changes.

Are there ready-made size presets for social media?

Yes, presets are included for Instagram, Facebook, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, WhatsApp, Pinterest and common print and screen sizes.

Does resizing remove EXIF or metadata?

By default, EXIF data such as camera details and location is stripped automatically during resizing for smaller files and better privacy.

Can I lock the aspect ratio while resizing?

Yes. Enable Lock Aspect Ratio and entering a width automatically calculates the matching height, and vice versa.

Which format should I choose — JPG, PNG or WEBP?

Use JPG for photographs, PNG for graphics that need transparency or sharp edges, and WEBP when you want the smallest file size with modern browser support.

Do I need to sign up or install anything?

No account, download or browser extension is required. Everything runs directly on this page.

Can I use this tool on mobile?

Yes, the resizer is fully responsive and works on phones and tablets, including pasting images from your clipboard.

Will the resized image have a watermark?

Never. OttLoop does not add watermarks or branding to any file you resize.

What is Smart Resize?

Smart Resize automatically applies high quality resampling and picks sensible output settings so the resized image looks sharp without you adjusting every option manually.

Can I convert the image format while resizing?

Yes. Choose an output format such as JPG, PNG, WEBP or AVIF and OttLoop will convert and resize the image in a single step.
